Reading Njáls saga on the T-bane

May 2026 · 6 min

What does a thirteenth-century blood feud sound like at eight in the morning, on the line to Blindern? I've been carrying the Íslendingasögur to class in a canvas tote, reading the feuds between stops, and finding that the medieval and the everyday keep folding into one another.

There is a rhythm to the sagas that survives the commute — a plainness, a refusal to explain a feeling — and I keep thinking about how much of my own life I could tell in that register.

My professor keeps reminding us that the sagas were written down generations after the events they describe, by people negotiating exactly what kind of past they wanted. That distance feels very familiar to a graduate student two continents from home.
